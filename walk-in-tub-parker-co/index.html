Parker's homes are, on the whole, newer and roomier than the metro average, and their bathrooms show it: big primary baths, garden tubs, glass showers. Spacious does not always mean safe, though. Here is how Parker homeowners typically make a large bathroom work for aging well.

The trouble with garden tubs

Large soaking tubs set into a deck or platform look inviting, but for an older bather they combine several risks: a step up onto the deck, a long reach over a wide rim, a low tub floor with nothing to hold, and a slippery exit. Many people with these tubs simply stop using them. If that is you, the space is an opportunity.

Glass showers and curbs

Frameless glass showers with a curb and a swinging door are common in Parker primary baths. For aging in place, check three things: the curb height, the door width, and whether there is anywhere to sit. A curb of several inches is a trip hazard for someone with a shuffling gait or a walker. A narrow glass door may not clear a walker or a shower chair. Converting to a low-threshold entry with a wider opening and a fold-down bench is often the most valuable change in the whole house.

Parker Senior Center

The Parker Senior Center at 10675 Longs Way is a nonprofit that has operated at its current location since 1985, serving adults 50 and older with activities, shared meals, outings and lifelong learning. Douglas County's Seniors' Council also offers educational activities through the Parker Senior Center. For many people, it is where they first hear about practical topics like home safety and fall prevention.

Thinking about resale

Parker homeowners often ask whether removing a garden tub hurts resale. We cannot predict any particular buyer, but here is how to keep options open: if you replace a garden tub with a walk-in tub, keep a standard tub or shower elsewhere in the house; if you replace it with a large walk-in shower, choose finishes that look like a modern spa shower rather than a clinical one. A well-designed curbless shower appeals to many buyers, not only older ones.

Practical notes for Parker installs

  • Measure the garden tub's full footprint including the deck, not just the basin.
  • Note whether the tub's motor or plumbing access panel is in a side wall or a closet.
  • Check the hot water capacity; larger homes sometimes have two water heaters, or a tank sized for a big soaking tub.
  • If the primary bath is upstairs, photograph the staircase and any landing.
  • Check HOA requirements for contractor parking and dumpsters.

Permits: Parker is an incorporated town with its own building rules; Denver's do not apply. Your installer handles permits where required.

Where grab bars go in a large primary bath

In a big bathroom, the distances between fixtures are part of the risk: more open floor to cross with wet feet and nothing to hold. When planning, think about the path, not only the fixtures:

  • A bar at the entry to the tub or shower, and one inside.
  • A bar beside the toilet, or a toilet safety frame if the wall is far away.
  • A bench or chair where you dry off, so you are not standing on one foot.
  • Non-slip flooring or a rubber-backed mat along the route from shower to vanity.
  • A light that comes on automatically at night.

Large bathrooms also tend to have large windows and bright, hard surfaces. Glare on a glossy floor can hide a puddle, so a matte, slip-resistant finish near the tub or shower is worth considering if flooring is being replaced at the same time.

Parker questions

Is a walk-in tub smaller than my garden tub?

Usually its footprint is smaller, though it is deeper. That can free up space for storage, a bench or a wider walkway.

Can my glass shower be made accessible?

Often. Lowering or removing the curb, widening the opening and adding a seat and grab bars are common changes. The details depend on the floor and drain.

Who can attend the Parker Senior Center?

The center describes its programs as serving adults 50 and older. Contact the center for membership details.

Can a walk-in tub fit in the space of a corner garden tub?

Often, because a walk-in tub usually has a smaller footprint. The leftover space can become storage or part of a larger shower. Measure the whole deck and send photos.
