Why we carry more than one brand

No single walk-in tub suits every Denver bathroom. A 1950s ranch in Englewood with a 60-inch alcove and a narrow hallway needs something different from a newer Castle Rock home with a roomy primary bath, and a bather who transfers from a wheelchair needs something different again. Each manufacturer makes its own choices about door design, seat height, sizing and options. Carrying four brands lets us match the tub to the person and the room instead of fitting the person to whatever one factory makes.

It also keeps the conversation honest. When we only have one product to sell, every answer starts to sound like that product. With four, we can say plainly, “This one has the door you need,” or “That one will not fit through your bathroom door,” and mean it.

Hydro Dimensions

Hydro Dimensions is a California maker that builds its walk-in tubs to order at its facility in Temecula and sells them through approved dealers rather than directly to the public. According to one industry review, its founders began in the specialty bathtub business in 1983 and formed their own company in 1993.

Where it tends to fit: because tubs are made to order, Hydro Dimensions is often worth a look when a household wants a particular combination of size, door side and options. The flip side of made-to-order is lead time, so ask about the expected build and delivery window when you get your quote, especially if you are planning around a hospital discharge or a family visit.

Ella's Bubbles

Aging Safely Baths & More is an Ella's Bubbles dealer. Ella's is known for offering both inward-swing and outward-swing doors, which matters more than most people expect:

  • Inward-swing doors open into the tub, so they need little or no clear floor space in front of it. They suit tighter bathrooms.
  • Outward-swing doors open into the room. Ella's own catalog notes they leave more room inside the tub but need more space in the bathroom for the door to open.
  • U-shaped and L-shaped doors. Ella's outward-swing line includes U-shaped doors and an L-shaped door used on its “Transfer” models. The L-shaped opening is designed to make getting in and out easier, including for people moving across from a wheelchair.

Where it tends to fit: households that need a specific door style for the bather's mobility or the room's layout, and bathers who transfer from a wheelchair or walker and want a wider opening.

Bestbath and RANE by Bestbath

Bestbath is a manufacturer in Caldwell, Idaho, whose roots go back to 1969. Its range includes showers, shower pans, walk-in tubs, grab bars and shower seats, and its products are used in homes as well as hospitals and senior living communities.

RANE by Bestbath is Bestbath's walk-in tub line. A 2025 trade-press profile described RANE tubs as designed for healthcare settings, with low entry thresholds, wide doors, ergonomic shapes, bariatric options, and manufacturing in the USA.

Name confusion: if you search online you may also find an older, unrelated company called Rane that was based in Tennessee. When we say RANE, we mean the line made by Bestbath in Idaho.

Where it tends to fit: households that want a tub designed with clinical and caregiving use in mind, people who need a bariatric model, and buyers who prefer a U.S.-made tub.

Meditub

Meditub is a walk-in tub brand sold under Spa World Corp., a company that also makes other bath and spa products. Its lineup includes standard walk-in tubs, low-profile and step-in models, bariatric tubs and wheelchair-accessible models. Meditub tubs are widely sold through home-improvement retailers and online stores, so you may already have seen them while researching.

Where it tends to fit: buyers who want to compare a broad range of standard sizes and styles, including low-profile designs for bathers who find a tall tub wall hard to manage.

How we help you choose between them

Brand names matter less than a handful of practical questions. These are the ones we work through on almost every Denver-area call:

  1. Will it get into the bathroom? Measure the bathroom door, hallway turns and stairs. Some tubs that fit the alcove will not make the trip. Our fit check walks through it.
  2. Which way should the door open? An outward-swing door needs open floor in front of the tub; an inward-swing door does not but takes up room inside.
  3. How does the bather sit down and stand up? Seat height, grab bar placement and the width of the opening matter more than jets.
  4. Is a bariatric or extra-wide model needed? Not every brand offers the same sizes, so this narrows the list quickly.
  5. Which comfort features are worth it? Heated seats, fast drains, air or water jets and handheld showers vary by model. See features explained.
  6. Can your water heater keep up? Bigger tubs need more hot water per bath. We check this before recommending a size.

A word about warranties

Warranty terms differ by manufacturer and often by model and component (shell, door seal, pumps, heater). They are also separate from the installer's workmanship coverage. Before you sign anything with anyone, ask for the manufacturer's written warranty for the exact model you are buying and the installer's workmanship terms in writing. We are glad to show you both for any tub we quote.

Brand questions

Is one walk-in tub brand safer than the others?

Safety depends far more on fit than on the name on the tub: a door that suits the bather, a comfortable seat height, grab bars where they are needed and a slip-resistant floor. A well-matched model from any of these four brands, installed correctly, will be safer than a poorly matched one from another.

Will any of these tubs fit where my current bathtub is?

Many walk-in tubs are made to replace a standard bathtub in its alcove, but exact dimensions vary by brand and model. Measure the alcove and the path into the bathroom first, then we can tell you which models fit.

Why does the brand affect how long I wait?

Some tubs are built to order and others ship from stock, and availability changes. Ask for the expected delivery window for the exact model in your written quote.
