Do you need a permit for a walk-in tub in Loveland?

Often, yes, and it depends on what changes behind the wall. The City of Loveland Building Division says a building permit is required for new construction, additions or alterations. Residential permit packets go by email to eplan-res@cityofloveland.org rather than through a walk-up counter.

The city's own monthly permit log is the clearest guide to what Loveland treats as permitted work. The February 2024 list includes residential permits for a replaced water heater, a replaced shower valve, and an "Alteration to Bathroom" that swapped a shower, toilet and sink. Each was issued under the 2021 International Residential and Plumbing Codes and the 2023 National Electrical Code, with field inspections.

Will your water heater fill a walk-in tub safely?

This is the question we ask on almost every Northern Colorado call, because the answer shapes both the tub choice and the price. A walk-in tub is deeper than a standard tub, and the bather sits in it while it fills and drains. Two things have to be true at once: there has to be enough hot water to fill it, and the water reaching the bather cannot be scalding.

The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission urges households to set water heaters to 120°F and says most tap-water scald victims are older adults and children under five. Its burn-time figures show why a few degrees matter so much:

So we plan around 120°F at the tap, an anti-scald mixing valve at the tub, and a heater big enough to fill the tub you pick. When the existing tank is small, the honest options are a shallower model, an inline heater on the tub, or a heater upgrade, and the quote prices each one. Who helps older adults in Loveland?

Chilson Senior Center

700 E. Fourth St., Loveland · 970-962-2783 · Mon to Fri, 8am to 5pm

The city's senior center runs daily SilverSneakers classes, a Volunteers of America lunch for people 60 and older (suggested $3 donation, reserve at 970-472-9630 by 11am the business day before), and a $20 Senior Activity Card for anyone 62 and over. It is a good place to ask what other families did about their bathrooms.

Larimer County Office on Aging

The Area Agency on Aging for Larimer County · 970-498-7750

The county's aging office provides programs for older adults and caregivers directly and through contracted community providers. The statewide Aging and Disability Resource line is 1-844-265-2372.

Volunteers of America Handyperson Program

Larimer County · 970-658-1233

Free safety repairs for limited-income seniors and adults with disabilities in Larimer County, including grab bars and handrails. If a full tub replacement is more than the budget allows, grab bars from this program are a sensible first step.

Larimer Home Improvement Program

Administered by the Loveland Housing Authority · 970-635-5931

Low- or no-interest repair loans for income-qualified households. A 2022 Senior Access Points listing described accessibility modifications as eligible, with a $24,999 cap for single-family homes at the time. Call to confirm the current terms before you plan around it.

Can Medicare, Medicaid or the VA help pay?

Original Medicare generally does not cover walk-in tubs. Health First Colorado members on the EBD, BI, CMHS or CIH waivers may qualify for the Home Modification benefit, capped at $14,000 and approved through a case manager. Aging Safely Baths explains that process step by step in its Colorado Medicaid waiver bathroom guide. Eligible veterans may qualify for VA HISA: up to $6,800 lifetime for a service-connected condition (or a 50% or higher rating) and $2,000 otherwise. Our paying for a walk-in tub guide covers each option, and we help you check what applies.

No bathing product eliminates the risk of falls; products are designed to reduce risk when installed and used properly.

How a Loveland install works

  1. Call or send photos. Tell us who will bathe, how they move today, and send photos of the tub, the faucet wall and the bathroom door.
  2. Written, itemized quote. Two or three models that fit, with the tub, options, permits and labor listed. No high-pressure sales visit.
  3. Deposit and technician site visit. A factory-certified technician visits to measure, photograph, check the water heater and electrical, and confirm the door side.
  4. Install day. The old tub comes out, the new one goes in, and the door seal, fill and drain are tested. Most installs take one day.
  5. Walkthrough. Everyone who will use or help with the tub learns the door, controls and drain before the crew leaves.
