Using a walk-in tub day to day

How long does a walk-in tub take to fill and drain?

It depends on the tub's capacity, your home's water pressure and the drain design. Filling is limited by how fast your faucet delivers water; draining by the tub's drain and your home's drain line. Fast-drain models are designed to shorten the wait. Ask for the manufacturer's stated fill and drain times for the specific model and remember that real-world times vary by home.

Do I get cold waiting for the tub to drain?

Some people do, which is why heated seats or backrests and fast drains are popular. Keeping the bathroom warm and draping a towel over your shoulders while the tub drains also helps.

Can two people use a walk-in tub at the same time?

Most walk-in tubs are designed for one bather. A few larger two-seat models exist, but they need a bigger footprint than a standard alcove.

Can I still take a shower in a walk-in tub?

Most installations include a handheld shower so you can rinse while seated. You can also keep a wall-mounted shower head above the tub if the surround is designed for it, though showering while standing in any tub carries more risk than sitting.

What happens if the power goes out during a bath?

Jets and heaters stop without power. On many models the door and a gravity drain are mechanical, but pump-assisted drains and some controls depend on electricity, so ask exactly how your model behaves in an outage before you buy.

Is it hard to keep clean?

The shell cleans like any acrylic or gelcoat tub. Jetted models need periodic line purging with a manufacturer-approved cleaner, and the door seal should be wiped and inspected regularly.

Is a walk-in tub safe for someone with balance problems?

For many people it is safer than a standard tub, because the bather sits at chair height instead of lowering to the tub floor and steps over a low threshold instead of a high wall. It is not right for everyone; someone who cannot sit and stand safely from a chair may be better served by a roll-in shower and a shower chair. Ask the bather's doctor or an occupational therapist.

How hot should the water be?

Comfortably warm, not hot, and checked by hand or thermometer before the bather gets in. Anti-scald valves limit the maximum temperature. People with diabetes, heart conditions, or reduced sensation should ask their doctor about safe soaking temperatures and times.

Your home and property

Will a walk-in tub hurt my home's resale value?

It depends on the buyer. Some buyers value an accessible bathroom; others prefer a standard tub. If resale is a near-term concern and there is only one full bathroom, a walk-in or roll-in shower tends to appeal to a wider range of buyers. We would rather tell you that honestly than sell you the wrong fixture.

Can I put a walk-in tub in a rental or in a parent's apartment?

You need the owner's written permission. Some landlords and senior communities welcome accessibility changes; others have strict rules. Ask before measuring. If you are a tenant with a disability, federal fair housing rules address reasonable modifications, and an advocacy organization can explain how they apply.

My HOA has rules about contractors. Is that a problem?

Usually not, but check first. HOAs and condo boards commonly set work hours, parking rules, elevator reservations and insurance requirements for contractors. Give us the requirements and we plan around them.

Can a walk-in tub go on a second floor?

Often yes. A filled walk-in tub plus a bather is heavy, so the installer should confirm the floor framing is suitable, and the stair route has to be wide enough to carry the tub up. Many newer Denver-area homes have upstairs primary bathrooms, so this comes up regularly.

What if my home has an older cast-iron tub?

Cast-iron tubs are very heavy, and installers often break them into pieces in place to remove them safely. That is part of a normal removal and is one reason removal is a line item on a quote.

Timing and logistics

Do you install in winter?

Yes. Installation happens indoors, so the season mostly affects the walkway. Please clear snow and ice from the driveway, walk and steps before the crew arrives, since they will be carrying a heavy tub.

Should I install before or after a planned hip or knee surgery?

Many people prefer to have it in place before surgery so the bathroom is ready for recovery. Ask your surgeon or therapist what bathing restrictions to expect, and plan the install date with enough margin.

I live outside the Denver metro. Can you still help?

Yes. We are based in Denver and install across the metro; the company also ships supply-only to all 50 states and to Canada for customers who have their own installer. See our service area page.

Money and paperwork

Does Medicare pay for a walk-in tub?

Original Medicare generally does not cover walk-in tubs or bathroom remodeling. Some Medicare Advantage plans offer limited home safety benefits, so ask your plan directly. Our paying-for-it guide lists other options.

Can a walk-in tub be a tax deduction?

Possibly, if its main purpose is medical care. IRS Publication 502 explains how medical home improvements are counted, including subtracting any increase in home value. Consult a tax professional about your situation.

What documents should I keep?

The itemized quote and invoice, the tub's spec sheet and owner's manual, both warranty documents, any permit records, and, if relevant, a doctor's note explaining the medical reason. Keep them together; you will want them for warranty, tax or grant questions.
